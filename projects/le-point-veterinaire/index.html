Back to projects Le Point Vétérinaire.fr

A veterinary product combining information, tools, and daily operational support.

Le Point Vétérinaire.fr was built to give veterinarians a single mobile product for professional news, training content, practical tools, directories, and reference data. The value came from bringing multiple everyday needs together in a product that stayed useful under regular professional use.

Things Are Moving

Native iOS development

March 2017 - September 2018

Built the application in Swift with responsibility for feature implementation, content access, and user experience across veterinary information, training, and operational utility layers.

  • Delivered a native iOS product that combined professional information, practical tools, and learning content for veterinarians.
  • Worked on features, content access patterns, and user experience in a multi-surface professional app.
  • Helped unify reference data, tools, and regular updates into one mobile workflow.
Veterinarians specialized professional audience with recurring information needs
Tools + directories + data operational utility layered into the product
iOS native mobile execution centered on practical professional use
Product

The app had to support both reading and action.

Professional news and training content were important, but the product also needed to function as a place to find tools, directories, and actionable reference material.

Information and training together

The app unified real-time news, training content, and digital publications so veterinarians could move between staying informed and learning in one product.

  • The product needed to support both quick consultation and deeper study.
  • Different content types had to remain coherent inside the same interface.

Practical utility layer

Directories, calculations, decision-support elements, and management tools made the application more than a publication reader.

  • Utility was central to daily professional value.
  • The product had to feel actionable, not just informative.

A daily professional companion

The broader design goal was to make the app feel worth revisiting as part of real veterinary work rather than as a static content library.

  • Repeat use shaped the product decisions.
  • The app needed to remain useful beyond one content session.
Reference

Reference data and tools had to stay accessible on mobile.

Veterinary drug data, supplier and professional directories, and supporting tools created a more operational product surface that had to remain usable on a phone.

Veterinary drug information

The product included reference data that needed to stay discoverable and readable inside a constrained mobile interface.

  • Reference depth shaped navigation and presentation choices.
  • The interface had to respect both speed and precision.

Operational tools

Calculation tools, directories, and decision-support functions made the app relevant to practical professional work, not only to reading.

  • The app served operational needs as well as editorial ones.
  • Practical features improved the product's recurring value.

Unified access

A core challenge was keeping these different resources reachable through one product structure that remained understandable over time.

  • The information architecture had to absorb complexity.
  • Consistency was essential to repeated use.
Execution

Native delivery kept the product grounded in real usage.

Swift-based implementation helped translate a complex professional surface into an app that stayed responsive, clear, and maintainable.

Feature implementation with context

Building features for the app meant understanding how information, tools, and workflows fit together instead of treating each screen in isolation.

  • Implementation stayed close to product logic.
  • The app benefited from decisions made at the system level.

Content access and usability

Making content and tools easy to reach was as important as adding them in the first place, especially for repeat professional use.

  • Usability was part of the product promise.
  • Mobile constraints had to support speed, not hinder it.

A coherent professional workflow

The product worked best when it felt like one dependable workflow surface rather than a stack of unrelated resources.

  • Coherence was a technical and product responsibility.
  • The strongest implementation choices supported recurring work habits.
