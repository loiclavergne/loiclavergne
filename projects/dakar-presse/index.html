Back to projects Dakar Presse

A focused press product for readers in Senegal and abroad.

Dakar Presse was designed to give readers access to Senegalese daily newspapers on mobile, both locally and across the diaspora. The product needed to feel simple, dependable, and well adapted to a geographically distributed audience with recurring reading habits.

Vialife

Project management, product design, and iOS development

November 2015 - July 2022

Owned the application across product design, project management, and iOS implementation on top of a custom Swift reading framework, with a focus on subscriptions, notifications, offline reading, and dependable daily access.

  • Built a focused mobile reading product for Senegalese newspapers and an international reader base.
  • Handled product design, project leadership, and native iOS implementation end to end.
  • Prioritized secure subscription flows, offline reading, and reliable access for a distributed audience.
Daily press product rhythm shaped by recurring newspaper reading
Diaspora audience served readers in Senegal and abroad
Subscriptions + alerts core engagement and monetization model
Audience

The product had a clear audience and a clear daily use case.

Unlike a broad multi-title kiosk, Dakar Presse was focused on a specific national press market and on readers who wanted dependable access to familiar daily content.

Readers in Senegal and abroad

The app served local readers as well as Senegalese communities living elsewhere, which made access reliability and mobile convenience especially important.

  • The product had to bridge geography without losing clarity.
  • Diaspora usage shaped the value proposition directly.

A daily reading habit

Because the product centered on daily newspapers, freshness, repeat visits, and a clear issue-based reading flow mattered more than novelty.

  • The product rhythm followed the cadence of the press itself.
  • Readers needed fast access to current and recent editions.

Focused product scope

The app benefited from being tightly scoped around a concrete audience and a recognizable content need.

  • The product could stay direct and purposeful.
  • Focus improved both UX and operational decisions.
Experience

The reading experience had to remain simple and trustworthy.

Subscription access, issue discovery, notifications, and offline reading all needed to support a daily-use product without making the app feel heavy or complicated.

Subscription-centered access

Flexible subscriptions were part of the core experience, so the purchase and renewal logic had to feel credible and easy to understand.

  • Access models had to match real reader behavior.
  • Trust in the subscription flow was part of product quality.

Notifications and cover discovery

Scheduled notifications and daily cover discovery helped connect the product to a recurring reading routine instead of a one-time download model.

  • The app needed to re-enter the reader's day naturally.
  • The front page remained a meaningful entry point.

Offline reliability

Offline reading helped the product stay useful across variable connection conditions and different usage contexts.

  • Dependability mattered as much as visual polish.
  • Mobile reading had to remain practical outside perfect connectivity.
Execution

End-to-end ownership kept the product coherent.

The combination of product design, project leadership, and iOS implementation helped keep the product focused while it evolved over time.

Design and implementation together

Design work in Sketch and iOS development in Swift were closely connected, which helped the final product stay practical and consistent.

  • The product benefited from fewer translation gaps.
  • Visual and technical choices could evolve together.

Custom reading foundation

The application relied on a proprietary reading framework rewritten in Swift to provide a stronger base for content presentation and usability.

  • The reading layer was a product differentiator.
  • A reusable foundation improved long-term technical control.

Reliable product operation

The broader goal was not just to ship features, but to keep the application dependable for a real audience returning day after day.

  • Operational reliability was part of the public product promise.
  • The strongest decisions were the ones that supported repeated use over time.
