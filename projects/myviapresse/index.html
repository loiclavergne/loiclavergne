Product

A consumer reading product had to stay simple despite a large catalog.

#

The application served magazine and newspaper readers directly, so catalog clarity, reading continuity, and purchase confidence had to coexist in one mobile experience.

Large catalog, clear access

The product exposed hundreds of titles while still needing to feel approachable for discovery, sampling, and repeat reading.

  • Catalog breadth could not overwhelm the product surface.
  • Discovery and direct purchase had to coexist cleanly.

Reading continuity

Offline reading and synchronized access across devices were part of making the product feel dependable instead of transactional.

  • The value of a press app continues after the purchase moment.
  • Continuity matters for recurring reader habits.

Editorial commerce

The application had to support both free discovery moments and paid conversion through individual purchases or subscriptions.

  • Sampling and monetization were both first-class product concerns.
  • The experience needed to feel commercial without becoming heavy.
Engineering

The project crossed several major implementation phases.

#

A meaningful part of the work was preserving product continuity while modernizing the stack, which made technical transitions a central product responsibility.

From Titanium to Swift

The application started from a hybrid Titanium implementation and later moved into native Swift as the product and quality expectations evolved.

  • The transition created room for better performance and maintainability.
  • Native implementation improved control over the reading experience.

A full rewrite when needed

Later versions required a more complete rewrite in newer Xcode and Swift environments so the app could stay viable over time.

  • The product was not kept alive through small patches alone.
  • Durability sometimes required deeper technical resets.

Proprietary reading framework

A custom Swift reading framework gave the product a more consistent and performant base for content presentation and offline use.

  • The reader was a core capability, not just a screen.
  • Reusable internal tooling improved the long-term technical base.
Monetization

Commerce flows had to be credible, simple, and durable.

#

Subscriptions and single-issue purchases were not peripheral features. They were central to product viability, which meant the in-app commerce layer had to feel trustworthy and operationally stable.

Single issues and subscriptions

The app supported both one-off purchases and auto-renewable subscriptions, which required a careful balance between flexibility and clarity.

  • Different reader behaviors needed distinct purchase paths.
  • The product had to stay understandable around pricing and access.

Store-driven product constraints

Implementing monetization meant working with the realities of in-app purchase systems, entitlement handling, and long-term maintainability.

  • Commerce was tied to technical correctness as much as UI.
  • The business model depended on reliable operational flows.

Consumer product durability

Because the application lived for years, monetization and reading systems both had to survive stack changes, catalog growth, and product iteration.

  • Longevity was part of the engineering challenge.
  • The strongest technical choices were the ones the product could keep carrying forward.
