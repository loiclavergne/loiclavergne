Product

A driver utility product grounded in real territory data.

The product is meant to be practical first: routing, fuel, charging, parking, nearby services, and place discovery all need to feel reliable in a single everyday experience.

Driver utility first

The app is designed around frequent driving needs rather than novelty, which keeps the product surface concrete and useful.

  • Routing and mobility services stay central.
  • The user value is immediate and recurring.

Territory-aware information

Public and partner datasets are combined to expose localized information such as fuel shortages, service availability, and nearby points of interest.

  • Reliability depends on aggregation and contextual presentation.
  • The French territory is part of the product's specificity.

Navigation without lock-in

The product can guide users toward relevant destinations while still handing off to their preferred navigation tools when appropriate.

  • The experience stays helpful without forcing one navigation pattern.
  • Interoperability matters as much as interface polish.
Engineering

The mobile foundation had to evolve while the product kept shipping.

The role began with direct technical ownership of the application and later widened into architecture stewardship across a growing delivery surface.

Foundations first

Early work focused on structuring the mobile codebase so the product could keep growing without accumulating delivery friction.

  • Code organization and ownership had to become clearer.
  • The technical baseline needed to support iteration speed.

Architecture evolution

A significant part of the work was guiding the application toward an MVVM architecture with SwiftUI while keeping maintainability and team readability high.

  • Architecture changes were tied to real delivery needs.
  • The goal was durability, not framework fashion.

Tooling and quality

Builds, integration practices, and development workflows were treated as part of product quality rather than as side concerns.

  • Continuous integration and tooling help stabilize delivery.
  • Maintainability is part of release predictability.
Delivery

The project became a coordination problem as much as a coding problem.

As the role widened, the work shifted toward keeping roadmap, backend dependencies, mobile execution, and shared standards aligned across teams.

Roadmap and engineering together

Technical direction stayed connected to product priorities so architecture decisions could support delivery rather than drift away from it.

  • Risks and dependencies stay visible earlier.
  • Planning becomes more credible when engineering is fully in the loop.

Cross-platform alignment

The work now includes coordination across iOS and Android so product execution stays coherent even when implementation details differ.

  • Shared standards reduce unnecessary divergence.
  • The coordination layer matters as the product grows.

Frontend and backend rhythm

A meaningful part of the role is keeping frontend and backend delivery in sync so product changes remain feasible, maintainable, and testable.

  • API integration is part of product planning.
  • Execution quality depends on inter-team rhythm.
