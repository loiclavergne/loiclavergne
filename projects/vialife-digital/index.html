Back to projects Vialife Digital

A digital publishing platform built across products, formats, and channels.

Vialife Digital was designed to help press publishers manage, distribute, and evolve their digital content across web, mobile, audio, and voice surfaces. The work combined technical direction, product design, and business alignment around a platform rather than a single app.

Directed the design, development, and technical direction of Vialife Digital while contributing to business development and product strategy for publishers moving beyond traditional PDF distribution.

  • Led the platform across APIs, web and native readers, content transformation, audio generation, and voice integrations.

Platform

The product was a system, not a single interface.

The value came from connecting content ingestion, transformation, distribution, and reading experiences into one coherent platform that publishers could actually operate.

Unified publishing platform

Vialife Digital brought together APIs, readers, catalog management, and distribution surfaces so publishers could work from a more consistent digital base.

  • The platform connected operational and end-user layers.
  • The product vision was broader than one channel or one device.

Content transformation

A key part of the platform was turning source material such as PDFs into richer digital formats that could support better reading and distribution experiences.

  • Article extraction helped move beyond static facsimile reading.
  • Transformation capabilities created room for more accessible formats.

Native and web reading surfaces

The platform included both web and mobile reading experiences, plus native frameworks intended to package the platform capabilities into reusable delivery tools.

  • The reader experience had to stay coherent across surfaces.
  • Reuse mattered for long-term product economics.
Expansion

The platform pushed beyond classic reading apps.

The work explored how press content could be repurposed for audio, voice assistants, and emerging consumption formats without losing control of the publishing system underneath.

Audio generation

The platform explored automated audio transformations so publishers could extend their catalogs into listening experiences with less manual production effort.

  • Machine learning entered the product through practical transformation problems.
  • Audio was treated as an extension of publishing, not a disconnected experiment.

Voice assistant integrations

Integrations with Siri, Alexa, and Google Assistant extended the distribution model into voice-driven usage patterns.

  • The same platform needed to serve different interaction models.
  • Content distribution had to stay consistent despite channel differences.

Forward-looking R&D

The roadmap also looked at augmented and virtual reality formats to stay ahead of changing reader behaviors and distribution opportunities.

  • Exploration stayed tied to product strategy.
  • R&D remained useful because it was connected to the platform's long-term direction.
Execution

Technical direction had to stay close to publisher reality.

The role combined architecture, product strategy, and business alignment, which meant the platform had to be technically sound and commercially useful at the same time.

Security and controlled access

Authenticated service-to-service communication, encrypted storage, and controlled access to digital assets were built into the platform rather than added later.

  • Security was part of the product promise to publishers.
  • Sensitive content distribution required stronger operational discipline.

Scalability and maintainability

The platform had to stay flexible enough to support multiple publisher contexts and delivery channels without becoming brittle.

  • Long-term evolution mattered more than short-term patchwork.
  • Architecture choices were shaped by platform lifespan.

Product and business together

Technical execution was continuously aligned with business development and publisher adoption so the platform could evolve in directions that were both feasible and valuable.

  • The role sat between engineering depth and commercial usefulness.
  • Platform strategy worked because it stayed tied to real publisher needs.

Engineering leadership for mobile products, architecture, and delivery.
