System

The challenge was scale through structure, not duplication.

The suite only worked if the common foundation stayed strong enough to carry multiple products without forcing every new application to become a rewrite.

A shared technical base

The applications were built on one templated architecture and one reading foundation so the core behaviors could be reused instead of rebuilt.

  • Core features needed to stay stable across brands.
  • Reuse was essential to delivery speed and long-term maintenance.

Variation without fragmentation

Each application still had to support its own access rules, audience, and context without breaking the coherence of the shared system.

  • Customization had to stay bounded.
  • The suite needed flexibility without dissolving into six unrelated apps.

Reading consistency

Catalog discovery, authentication, and reading flows needed to feel dependable regardless of the application's specific business model.

  • The reading experience was a common product promise.
  • Consistency mattered more than surface-level visual variation.
Product

Different access models shaped the design problem.

The suite covered corporate, hospitality, event-based, and more general distribution use cases, which meant product design had to be modular but still understandable.

Organizational distribution

Some applications were designed for companies, partners, or membership contexts where access came through organizational authentication rather than an individual storefront.

  • The product model changed with the client context.
  • Access logic shaped both UX and delivery requirements.

Hospitality and event usage

Other products targeted hotels, salons, or temporary event scenarios where convenience, lightweight onboarding, and controlled access mattered most.

  • The same reading core had to work in transient contexts.
  • The onboarding expectations differed from standard consumer apps.

A family of products, not one clone

The goal was not to stamp out copies mechanically, but to build a coherent family of products that could express different distribution strategies on top of the same foundation.

  • The architecture had to support product nuance.
  • Template work still needed product judgment.
Delivery

Ownership combined design, implementation, and operational clarity.

Because the suite lived across several products, the work demanded end-to-end ownership rather than isolated implementation tasks.

Project management and iOS delivery

The role covered coordination, specification, and native iOS implementation, which helped keep product intent and execution tightly connected.

  • The work moved from design decisions to shipped code directly.
  • Fewer handoff gaps meant more coherent outcomes.

Design with implementation awareness

Product design in Sketch was informed by the realities of the shared mobile framework, which helped keep the suite ambitious but realistic.

  • Design decisions stayed close to implementation constraints.
  • Shared components benefited from repeated product use.

Operational efficiency

A reusable suite only creates value if it also reduces delivery friction, release effort, and long-term maintenance costs across products.

  • The system had to compound over time.
  • Efficiency was part of the product strategy, not just an engineering preference.
